## Working rules

- Preserve original routes, useful content, keyword intent, and existing metadata by default. Document factual corrections and any substantive copy changes. No bulk rewrites merely to reach a length target or sound different.
- Keep the homepage H1 “Beautiful Window Treatments Fort Wayne Indiana.” Product categories own broad product selection; detail pages own individual treatments; blogs remain informational and word-for-word intact.
- Preserve the 17 city URLs during the migration. Do not merge pages because they contain similar keywords or because Semrush returns no rows. Consider consolidation separately using query/page history, links, and leads.
- Use the approved fonts, colors, compact buttons, full-width navigation, restrained sections, and mobile Call/menu header. Hero review badges and David's video stay on the homepage.
- No decorative material icons, generic town praise, invented local installations, fake offices, copied city-name swaps, em dashes in new copy, or reinstated buy-two-get-one offer.
- Do not require a photo of David measuring to proceed. Use approved existing assets sparingly and keep image replacement separate from page completion. Never label staged imagery as an actual client project.

Acceptance: working delivered enquiries, understood integration dependencies, reviewed content changes, verified tracking, and clear unresolved items rather than assumed completion.

## Phase 6: complete the migration checks and publish

1. Reconcile all 55 discovered live pages and the full 122-URL migration map. Preserve working routes. Validate proposed legacy redirects individually; do not apply the 55 suggested redirects blindly or send missing pages to the homepage.
2. Crawl the complete preview. Check response status, titles, H1s, canonical URLs, internal links and anchors, images, schema, and real 404 behavior. Verify exact original blog wording and titles again.
3. Test representative homepage, category, detail, city, blog, and Contact pages on narrow mobile, tablet, and desktop. Check menus, section navigation, disclosures, carousels, forms, phone links, and video playback on the deployed preview. Measure loading performance, particularly large images and third-party assets.
4. Publish a complete GitHub/Netlify review build with search exclusion intact. Inspect deployed behavior, not only local files. Prepare the production sitemap and indexation settings separately.
5. Before switching production, refresh the ranking/traffic baseline, save a rollback deployment, verify host/HTTPS/canonicals, preserve email/DNS records and tracking, and confirm form delivery. Remove preview noindex/header/robots restrictions only on the intended production environment; retain intentional utility-page exclusions.
6. After the cutover, inspect important URLs and submit the production sitemap in Search Console. Check indexation, traffic, rankings, and enquiries daily initially, then weekly through the first several weeks. Investigate sustained changes with comparable data instead of rewriting pages after a single fluctuating position.

No automated monitoring is scheduled by this plan. Missing account access or client facts should be tracked explicitly; independent build work continues where possible.
