Window treatments for the rooms you use most

We look at sunlight, moisture, and access before narrowing the choices to the products that make sense for your windows.

Faux wood blinds for busy rooms

Faux wood blinds offer a wood-like finish with better moisture resistance than real wood. They are an option for kitchens, bathrooms, and other rooms where easy care matters. For larger windows, we compare price, available sizes, and how easy the blind is to raise. Follow the selected product’s care directions when cleaning the slats.

Compare wood and faux wood blinds

Cellular shades for year-round comfort

Cellular shades trap air within their honeycomb pockets, helping insulate at the window. Compare single- and double-cell designs and the fabric that suits your light and privacy needs. We check the fit at each opening and discuss how you will raise and lower the shade. Performance depends on the shade, the fit, and the existing window.

Explore cellular shades

Roman shades for fabric and texture

Roman shades add a fabric finish to living rooms, dining rooms, and bedrooms. When raised, the material gathers into folds at the top. We compare fold styles, patterns, and available linings, then look at how the stack affects your daylight and view. Room-darkening fabric still needs suitable edge coverage where sleep is the priority.

Compare Roman shade styles

Solar shades for picture windows and sunrooms

Solar mesh filters sunlight and can reduce glare and UV exposure while allowing a view during the day. Compare openness levels in the room where you plan to use it. A tighter weave and a more open weave offer different balances of sun control and visibility. For privacy at night, consider another fabric or a separate layer.

Explore solar shades

Easier control across several windows

If you find yourself leaving a treatment open because it is difficult to reach, ask about motorized options. We can check which products fit the opening and compare remote controls, power sources, and access for charging. App and scheduling features depend on the selected system and accessories.

Shutter panels and frames

Shutter louvers need space to tilt, and panels need clearance to open. We check handles, trim, and nearby furniture, then compare frame styles and materials. Choose shutters for the way you want to use the room and care for the treatment.

Ranch and picture windows

A wide opening may need more than one blind or shade. We can compare the appearance and everyday operation of separate sections, including how their alignment looks when raised or lowered.

Exposed and rural windows

Tell us when direct sun reaches the room and which views you want to keep. For a country property, privacy still matters after dark, even when neighboring homes are farther away.

Are faux wood blinds lighter than wood blinds?

Not necessarily. Weight depends on the material and size, and faux wood can be heavier. We check the width and control options so the blind is practical to use.

Will solar shades give privacy at night?

Solar mesh can allow a view into a lit room after dark. If you need nighttime privacy, tell us before choosing the fabric so we can compare opaque shades or an additional covering.

Can I keep my existing curtains?

Often, yes. We will check the available depth and the position of the curtain hardware. Bring this up during the visit so the blind or shade is planned to work with the curtains.

What happens after installation?

We check the treatment’s operation and show you how to use and care for it. Contact David if you have questions afterward or want to discuss another room.
