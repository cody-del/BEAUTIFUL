Louver sizes

2.5 inch louvers - Traditional size. More louvers per panel create a classic look with finer lines. Better light control precision but more visible louvers when open throughout Monroeville and Harlan.

3.5 inch louvers - Most popular size. Good balance of view when open and coverage when closed. Works well on standard window sizes.

4.5 inch louvers - Wider louvers mean fewer per panel. More contemporary appearance with maximum view when open. Show more window when tilted. Popular in newer homes in Hoagland and Syracuse.

Wider louvers create a more open, modern look. Narrower louvers feel more traditional and provide finer light control adjustments.

Frame styles and configurations

L-frame - Frame on sides and top only, no bottom rail. Creates cleaner lines on windowsills. Popular in contemporary homes.

Tier-on-tier - Separate top and bottom sections operate independently. Upper section opens for light while lower stays closed for privacy.

Composite shutters vs other options

Composite vs wood shutters - Composite handles moisture better, costs less, needs less maintenance. Wood offers authentic appearance, can be stained, feels more substantial. Choose composite for bathrooms/kitchens or budget. Choose wood for dry locations where authenticity matters.

Composite shutters vs faux wood blinds - Shutters are permanent architectural elements that add home value. Blinds are window treatments that need eventual replacement. Shutters cost 3-4x more but last decades longer throughout Monroeville and Harlan.

Composite shutters vs cellular shades - Shutters offer superior durability, timeless style, and don't need replacement. Cellular shades provide better insulation, easier installation, and lower cost. Different solutions for different priorities.

Composite shutters vs vinyl blinds - Shutters are a premium investment with lasting value. Vinyl blinds are budget-friendly temporary solutions. Not comparable options - different markets entirely.

Common questions

How much do composite shutters cost?More than blinds or shades, less than wood shutters. Pricing depends on features and window sizes. Large windows cost more per square foot than small windows. We provide detailed pricing during your consultation.

Are they as good as wood shutters?Different, not better or worse. Composite handles moisture better and needs less maintenance. Wood looks more authentic and can be stained. Both are quality options for different situations.

Can they go in bathrooms?Yes - that's their primary advantage over wood. Composite shutters handle shower steam and humidity without warping or deteriorating.

Do they look fake?Quality composite shutters look great from normal viewing distance. Close inspection reveals they're not real wood, but most people won't notice the difference in everyday use.

Are they energy efficient?Yes - they provide insulation and reduce heat transfer. Not as insulating as cellular shades but better than most blinds. Expect modest energy savings.

Can I paint them later?Technically yes, but not recommended. Factory finish is superior to any repaint job. Choose your color carefully because changing it later is difficult.

What maintenance do they need?Very little. Dust or wipe clean occasionally. No refinishing, resealing, or special treatments needed. Among the lowest-maintenance window treatments available.

Are they better than faux wood blinds?Different purposes. Shutters are permanent architectural elements with lasting value. Faux wood blinds are removable window treatments. Shutters cost more but last longer and add more home value.

Do they add home value?Yes - plantation shutters are considered permanent upgrades that appeal to buyers. Composite shutters add value similar to wood shutters at lower cost.
