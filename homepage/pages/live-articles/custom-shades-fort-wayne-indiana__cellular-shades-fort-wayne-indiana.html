Single-cell vs double-cell

The cell construction affects performance and cost.

Single-cell (⅜" or ½" cells) - One layer of honeycomb pockets. Good insulation, lower cost. Works well for most applications. Popular choice for homes in Bluffton and Warsaw where budget matters.

Double-cell (⅜" or ½" cells) - Two layers of honeycomb pockets. Maximum insulation. Better for extreme temperature windows or rooms that never feel comfortable. Worth the upgrade for problem windows in older homes throughout Columbia City and Grabill.

Cell size matters too. Larger cells (½") provide more insulation but are more visible. Smaller cells (⅜") offer a more refined look with slightly less insulation. We'll show you the difference during your consultation.

What to know before choosing cellular shades

They're not the prettiest option. We're being honest - cellular shades excel at function over fashion. The honeycomb structure is visible. If aesthetics matter most, roman shades might be better.

Dusting the cells can be tricky. Use a vacuum with brush attachment or compressed air. The pockets can collect dust over time, especially in light colors.

Side gaps allow some light. Standard cellular shades have small gaps where they meet the window frame. For total blackout, you need side tracks (which we offer).

More expensive than roller shades. The energy savings justify the cost, but the upfront investment is higher. Quality insulation isn't cheap.

Stack height varies by cell size. Larger cells stack bulkier than smaller cells when raised. Plan for about 2-3 inches per foot of shade length.
