Louver sizes

2.5 inch louvers - Traditional size with classic proportions. More louvers per panel create finer lines. Better light control precision. Traditional look that works in period homes throughout Hoagland and Syracuse.

3.5 inch louvers - Most popular size. Versatile option that works with various architectural styles. Good balance of view when open and coverage when closed in New Haven and Leo-Cedarville.

4.5 inch louvers - Contemporary wider louvers. Fewer louvers per panel create cleaner, more modern lines. Maximum view when open. Popular in newer construction and modern homes.

Louver size is a personal preference based on your home's architecture and your aesthetic goals. Traditional homes often use narrower louvers, contemporary homes look best with wider louvers.

Wood shutters vs any shade option - Shutters provide superior durability, timeless style, and don't need replacement. Shades offer easier installation and lower cost. Different solutions for different priorities and budgets in Columbia City and Grabill.

Maintenance and durability

Cleaning - Wipe louvers with slightly damp cloth or use duster. Avoid excessive water on wood surfaces. Simple maintenance keeps them looking beautiful for decades in Hoagland and Syracuse.

Moisture considerations - Wood shutters should not be installed in high-moisture areas like bathrooms. Use composite shutters in those locations instead. Wood performs beautifully in normal humidity conditions.

Refinishing potential - Unlike composite, wood shutters can be repainted or restained if you want to change colors later. Adds flexibility for future design changes in New Haven and Leo-Cedarville homes.

Repairs - Louvers can be replaced if damaged. Tilt mechanisms can be repaired. Quality shutters are built to be maintained and repaired rather than replaced.

Wood shutters are low-maintenance architectural elements. Occasional dusting is typically all they need to maintain their beauty for decades.

Common questions

How much do wood shutters cost?Premium investment - the highest-cost window treatment option. Pricing depends on window sizes, wood type, finish, and features. Large windows and custom finishes cost more. We provide detailed pricing during your consultation.

Are they better than composite shutters?Different purposes. Wood offers authentic beauty and can be stained or refinished. Composite handles moisture better and costs less. Wood is the premium choice for dry locations where quality and authenticity matter most.

Can they go in bathrooms?No - moisture will damage wood shutters over time. Use composite shutters in bathrooms, kitchens, and other high-humidity areas instead.

Do they look better than composite?Wood has authentic grain and natural character that composite can't match. The difference is noticeable, especially with stained finishes. Wood feels more substantial and premium.

Are they energy efficient?Yes - solid wood construction provides excellent insulation. They reduce heat transfer through windows and help lower heating and cooling costs.

Can I change the color later?Yes - wood shutters can be repainted or restained if you want to change colors in the future. This flexibility is a key advantage over composite shutters.

What maintenance do they need?Very little. Dust or wipe clean occasionally. Keep away from excessive moisture. No refinishing needed unless you choose to change the color. Among the lowest-maintenance options available.

Do they add home value?Yes - significantly. Wood plantation shutters are considered premium upgrades that appeal strongly to home buyers. They're an investment that enhances resale value.

What's the difference between louver sizes?Smaller louvers (2.5") create traditional, classic look. Larger louvers (4.5") create contemporary, open feel. Medium louvers (3.5") work with most styles. Choose based on your home's architecture and personal preference.
