Single- and double-cell cellular shades

Cellular shades use air pockets to help slow heat transfer at the window. Double-cell designs have two layers of pockets, but the result also depends on the fabric, fit, and window. We can compare the options for colder rooms or exposed windows, along with light-filtering and room-darkening materials. Shades do not repair an air leak or damaged window.

Compare cellular shade options

Wood blinds beside existing finishes

Real wood blinds bring visible grain and a choice of stain or painted finishes. Compare samples beside the woodwork in your living room, dining room, or office. Tilting the slats adjusts light and privacy without raising the blind. We also check mounting depth, window width, and operation so the selected size is comfortable to use.

Explore blind materials

Solar shades for bright views

If afternoon sun reaches a screen or seating area, solar mesh can help reduce glare while preserving a daytime view. Compare openness and color against the light at that window. It is useful to think about evening privacy at the same time: solar mesh alone can allow visibility into a room when the indoor lights are on.

Explore solar shades

Roman shades for a fabric finish

Roman shades add texture or pattern, with fabric that gathers into folds as it rises. Compare a flatter style with softer folds and choose a lining for the room. A bedroom may need more light coverage than a dining room. We check how much glass the folded fabric covers when raised and where light could enter around the sides.

Plan around the opening and the room

Custom measurements are useful for an older home, a ranch, or a new build. The details vary from window to window.

Older trim and frames

We measure each opening and look for uneven depth, projecting handles, and woodwork you want to keep visible. Those details help determine whether to mount inside or outside the frame.

Windows facing open land

Compare glare and privacy at the times you use the room. A distant neighbor does not rule out nighttime privacy needs, and a wide view may make the size of the raised shade stack important.

Large or high openings

Discuss the control position before choosing a treatment. We check the available product sizes and whether separate shades, a different mounting position, or motorization would make operation easier.

How do single- and double-cell shades compare?

Double-cell designs have an additional layer of air pockets. Fabric, construction, fit, and the existing window also affect insulation. We can show you the available styles and explain their differences for your rooms.

Can motorized shades work without a smart-home setup?

Yes, compatible products can use a dedicated remote. If you also want an app, voice commands, or schedules, we can explain which accessories and connections are needed.

What if my window is hard to reach?

Tell us when arranging the consultation. We will discuss access for measuring and installation as well as how you will operate the finished treatment and reach its power source.
