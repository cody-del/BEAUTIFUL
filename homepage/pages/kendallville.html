Update the windows without overlooking the details

Each room has its own light and its own way of being used. We compare products with those differences in mind.

Cellular shades for bedrooms and living rooms

The honeycomb construction of cellular shades helps insulate at the window. Compare single- and double-cell styles, light-filtering and room-darkening fabrics, and controls for rooms where comfort matters. A proper fit is part of the decision. If the frame is damaged or drafty, a shade can help with comfort but will not repair the underlying window.

Compare cellular shades

Plantation shutters for a fitted finish

Shutters bring adjustable louvers and a visible frame to the opening. We measure depth and check that panels can swing clear of furniture and window hardware. Compare wood and composite materials with your room conditions, then look at finishes and louver sizes. The view with the louvers open matters as much as the closed appearance.

Explore plantation shutters

Solar and roller shades for daylight

Solar fabrics can reduce glare and UV exposure while allowing a daytime view. Roller shades also offer opaque and blackout materials for rooms that need more privacy. Compare the options in the actual light, and discuss evening use before settling on solar mesh. We can explain where an additional layer might be useful.

See roller shade fabrics

Roman shades for color and texture

Roman shades let you bring a fabric pattern or a softer texture into the room. We compare fold styles and linings, then check how much space the raised fabric will occupy. For an older window with decorative trim, mounting position can help balance the fabric coverage with the features you want to leave visible.

Explore Roman shade options

Plan a replacement, renovation, or new installation

The existing opening and work still to be done help determine when to measure and where to mount.

Replacing older treatments

Tell us what bothers you about the current covering. We can compare changes in fabric, controls, or mounting rather than simply ordering the same style again. Existing bracket holes do not always suit a new product.

Preserving original details

Bring up trim or other features you want to keep visible. We measure each opening and check depth and clearances, particularly where older frames are uneven or sizes vary across the room.

Finishing a new space

Let us know about windows, painting, or trim still in progress. You can begin comparing materials now, and we will take final measurements once the windows and trim are ready.

Questions before your Kendallville visit

Have another question about your windows?

Can I replace the coverings a few rooms at a time?

Yes. We can start with the windows that need attention first. Share the longer-term plan so we can compare colors and product choices that work together.

Can the new treatment use my old brackets?

Not always. Brackets and spacing vary by product. We check the mounting surfaces and explain what the new installation will need.

Which is easier to clean, blinds or shades?

Care depends on the material. We can compare the cleaning directions for the products you are considering, including whether they suit a kitchen or another area where dust or moisture is a concern.

How do I prepare for the consultation?

Make a note of the rooms you want to cover and any problems with light, privacy, or controls. You do not need final measurements. Tell us about access needs or unfinished renovation work when arranging the visit.
