Discuss your blinds or shades

Wood and faux wood blinds

Real wood and its finish need different handling from moisture-resistant faux wood. Water or harsh cleaners can damage some finishes. We consider the surface and manufacturer’s care instructions before selecting a method.

Mini, aluminum, vinyl, and vertical blinds

Dust and residue can gather across slats, vanes, and tracks. Bent parts, brittle material, and worn finishes should be identified before cleaning. Fabric vertical vanes require different care from vinyl.

Cellular and pleated shades

The folds, fabric, internal cords, and adhesives all matter. Honeycomb shades should not be treated like solid slats. We discuss the construction and any existing separation or fabric damage.

Roller, Roman, and solar shades

Fabrics, coatings, linings, and decorative folds affect what can be cleaned. A suitable method for one roller or Roman shade may not be suitable for another. Motorized controls also need consideration.

Ultrasonic blind cleaning

Ask David about ultrasonic cleaning for your blinds. The finish, fabric, adhesives, and working parts all affect whether it’s a suitable option. We’ll discuss the cleaning method and expected results with you first.

Keep the treatment
and its condition in mind

Cleaning and repair solve different problems. Let us know about both if your treatment is dirty and difficult to operate.

Care between cleanings

Follow the manufacturer’s directions for dusting and any low-suction vacuuming. Do not soak, steam, or apply household cleaners unless the product’s care instructions allow it.

Mechanical problems

Cleaning will not fix a broken cord, worn clutch, or damaged carrier. If the treatment will not operate properly, ask about blind and shade repair as part of the assessment.

Permanent wear or damage

Faded fabric, brittle parts, or peeling finishes may not improve with cleaning. If replacement makes more sense, compare new blinds and new shades before deciding.

Can every type of blind or shade be cleaned?

Not every treatment is suitable for every method. Material, finish, construction, age, and existing damage all matter. We discuss the individual product before confirming what can be done.

Do you offer ultrasonic blind cleaning?

Ask David which cleaning method suits your blinds or shades, including whether ultrasonic cleaning is an option. Fabric, real wood, and motorized treatments may need different care.

Can you remove stains and kitchen grease?

The result depends on the surface, the substance, and how long it has been there. Some buildup may be removable, while staining, fading, or finish damage may remain. Discuss expectations before agreeing to the work.

Do you take the blinds down and put them back up?

Ask what’s included in your quote, including taking the treatments down, transport, and rehanging them. We’ll explain where the cleaning will take place and who handles each step.

How often should blinds be professionally cleaned?

The room, material, and amount of buildup matter more than one fixed schedule. Follow the product’s regular care instructions, and ask about cleaning when dust or residue is difficult to manage safely.

How much does blind cleaning cost?

The number, size, material, condition, and handling of the treatments affect the quote. If you are considering a whole-home cleaning, tell us how many treatments you have so we can discuss them together.
