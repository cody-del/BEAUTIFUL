Cellular, solar, roller, and Roman shades handle light differently. Compare them for the way you use each room.

Cellular shades for window comfort

Honeycomb shades use fabric pockets to trap air at the window. Compare their cell construction and light control for bedrooms or rooms where comfort is a concern. A well-fitted covering can help manage light and heat transfer, but it does not fix a damaged or leaking window.

Solar and roller shades for everyday light

Solar screen fabric is worth comparing for glare on a television or work screen. Check the view and nighttime privacy as well as the daytime light. Roller fabrics range from light filtering to room darkening; we also discuss mounting and light gaps around the edges.

Roman shades for a softer finish

Roman shades gather into folds as they rise. They offer a fabric finish for a living room, dining space, or bedroom. Compare the lining and pattern, then check how much glass the folded fabric covers when raised.

Older windows, picture windows, and new construction

The original window frame and the space around it determine what can be fitted. We check those details during your New Haven visit.

Older frames and original trim

A shallow recess or uneven opening may change the mounting choice. We measure the available space and discuss how much trim you want visible. Bring up any planned window replacements or repairs before measurements are finalized.

Ranch and split-level layouts

Wide picture windows and sliding doors need treatments that are practical to move out of the way. We check the space where blinds or shades stack, the location of furniture, and access to door handles. Different openings can coordinate without using identical products.

Newer homes and high windows

Great rooms may combine tall windows with smaller openings nearby. Consider whether you want matching fabric, grouped operation, or separate privacy levels. For plantation shutters, compare panel arrangements and clearance as well as the wood or composite finish.
